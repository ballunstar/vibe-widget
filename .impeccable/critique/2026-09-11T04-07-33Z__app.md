## Design Specificity Verdict

VibeWidget feels authored where it matters most: the split-donut menu-bar gauge, Claude/ChatGPT accent pairing, monospaced quota figures, and family-specific widgets are unmistakable. The dashboard is less specific because it falls back to generic soft cards nested inside soft cards and repeats each quota in several visual forms.

The deterministic scan returned zero findings for `App`, `Widget`, and `Shared/UsageViews.swift`. Direct source evidence still found duplicate refresh controls, fixed-width desktop layouts, ambiguous empty/Online states, and eleven settings controls with hidden empty labels and no explicit accessibility labels. Browser overlays were not applicable because this is native SwiftUI rather than DOM-rendered UI.

## Overall Impression

The product has a strong compact-instrument identity but the expanded desktop experience does not yet make the primary question—how much usable quota remains, and when does it reset—as fast or trustworthy as the menu-bar icon promises.

## What's Working

- Centralized provider accents, real logos, monospaced figures, and shared gauge/bar primitives create strong cross-surface identity.
- Small, medium, and large widgets restructure content rather than merely scale it.
- Settings dependencies are visibly grouped and subordinate controls disable when their parent feature is off.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P0] The app can remove its only ordinary entry point.** A background-only app can disable its MenuBarExtra. Preserve the preference only with a safe recovery surface or clear safeguard. Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`.
2. **[P1] Empty, stale, and healthy states are not truthful.** No data can appear Online; missing reset data is described as all windows being full. Introduce honest empty/unknown wording and avoid equating no error with fresh data. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`.
3. **[P1] Settings controls are visually labeled but semantically anonymous.** Empty labels plus `.labelsHidden()` break control-label association, and icon-only refresh controls lack explicit accessible names. Add accessibility labels/values and selected-state semantics. Suggested command: `$impeccable audit`.
4. **[P2] The dashboard repeats data instead of sharpening the decision.** A quota appears as large text, a segmented bar, a second percentage in a ring, and inverse used copy; Refresh is duplicated as Sync now. Keep one dominant number and one graphical encoding, one Refresh action, and explicit 5-hour/Weekly labels. Suggested command: `$impeccable distill`.
5. **[P2] Desktop adaptation and compact-widget context are incomplete.** Dashboard and Settings enforce wide minimums; cards never collapse; the small widget hides which window supplied the limiting value. Add narrow/wide structures and window labels. Suggested command: `$impeccable adapt`.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Alex, power user:** Refresh appears more than once but has no consistent shortcut; prime space restates numbers rather than exposing the binding constraint.
- **Sam, accessibility-dependent:** empty-label controls, visual-only sidebar selection, very small support text, and unlabeled custom gauges make VoiceOver and low-vision use unnecessarily difficult.
- **Jordan, first-timer:** Online beside em dashes, Session without five-hour context, and every window is already full on missing reset data create a false mental model.

## Minor Observations

- The selected General pane is headed Settings instead of General.
- Preferences and Settings, plus Sync and Refresh, refer to the same destinations/actions.
- A prominent Done button implies deferred saving even though preferences apply immediately.
- Provider marketing subtitles consume space without helping quota interpretation.
- Custom Widget is too generic beside AI Usage.

## Questions to Consider

- Why can a menu-bar-only app hide its own menu bar?
- Is Online defensible for a cached snapshot assembled from local files and an intermittently available token?
- What if the dashboard behaved like a precise status instrument: one limiting number, one reset, one recovery action?
